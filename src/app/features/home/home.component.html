Kid Dynamite (1911–1963) was the stage name of Surinamese-Dutch tenor saxophonist Arthur Parisius, one of the most distinctive figures in post-war Dutch jazz. Born in Suriname, he came to the Netherlands as a young man and built his reputation in the jazz clubs of Amsterdam and Rotterdam.

A powerful, energetic player influenced by American tenor saxophonists such as Coleman Hawkins, Kid Dynamite became particularly popular during the 1940s and 1950s. He was a familiar presence at Amsterdam’s legendary Casablanca on the Zeedijk and played with musicians including Boy Edgar, Teddy Cotton and Cees Slinger. His music combined American swing and jazz with elements of Surinamese music, particularly kaseko—an unusual mixture at the time and an early example of what would later be called world music.

Kid Dynamite died in 1963, aged 52, following a car accident in Germany. He is now remembered as one of the pioneers of both Dutch jazz and Surinamese jazz in the Netherlands.
